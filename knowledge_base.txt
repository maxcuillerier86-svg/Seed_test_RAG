## TOMATO GROWING GUIDE
Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum) is one of the most popular home garden vegetables. Varieties include Cherry, Roma, Beefsteak, Heirloom, Brandywine, San Marzano, Grape, and Early Girl. Tomatoes need full sun (6-8 hours), well-drained soil rich in organic matter with pH 6.0-6.8, and 1-2 inches of water per week. Start seeds indoors 6-8 weeks before last frost. Transplant outdoors after danger of frost has passed. Plant deep — bury 2/3 of the stem as roots grow from buried stems. Space 24-36 inches apart. Use cages or stakes for support. Prune suckers on indeterminate varieties for larger fruit. Common problems: blossom end rot (calcium deficiency/inconsistent watering), hornworms, early blight, late blight, cracking. Harvest when fruit is fully colored and slightly soft. Days to harvest: 60-85 from transplant. Temperature: min 50°F, ideal 70-85°F. Companion plants: basil, carrots, marigolds. Avoid planting near: brassicas, fennel.

## LETTUCE GROWING GUIDE
Lettuce (Lactuca sativa) is a cool-season crop perfect for beginners. Varieties include Butterhead (Bibb, Boston), Romaine (Cos), Leaf (Red Leaf, Green Leaf, Oak Leaf), Iceberg, and Mesclun mixes. Lettuce needs partial to full sun (4-6 hours), loose well-drained rich soil with pH 6.0-7.0, and 1 inch of water per week. Surface sow seeds barely covering with 1/8 inch of soil. Germination in 2-10 days. Space 6-12 inches apart. Lettuce grows great in containers, window boxes, and as interplanting between larger crops. Use "cut and come again" method: harvest outer leaves while inner leaves continue growing. Full heads ready in 30-60 days. Bolts (goes to seed) in hot weather above 80°F. Grow in partial shade during summer or use shade cloth. Succession plant every 2 weeks for continuous harvest. Common problems: slugs, aphids, tip burn, bolting. Companion plants: carrots, radishes, strawberries, chives. Temperature: min 40°F, ideal 60-70°F.

## BASIL GROWING GUIDE
Basil (Ocimum basilicum) is a warm-season herb essential for cooking. Varieties include Sweet Basil (Genovese), Thai Basil, Purple Basil (Dark Opal), Lemon Basil, Cinnamon Basil, Holy Basil (Tulsi), and Greek Basil. Basil needs full sun (6-8 hours), rich well-drained soil with pH 6.0-7.0, and consistent moisture. Start seeds indoors 6-8 weeks before last frost or direct sow after frost. Germination in 5-10 days. Space 12-18 inches apart. Pinch growing tips regularly to encourage bushy growth and prevent flowering. Remove flower buds immediately as flowering reduces leaf flavor. Can grow indoors year-round on a sunny windowsill. Harvest from the top down, cutting just above a leaf pair. Days to harvest: 30-60. Temperature: min 50°F, ideal 70-80°F. Very frost sensitive — dies at first frost. Companion plants: tomatoes, peppers. Common problems: downy mildew, fusarium wilt, aphids.

## PEPPER GROWING GUIDE
Pepper (Capsicum annuum/chinense) varieties range from sweet to extremely hot. Types include Bell Pepper, Jalapeño, Cayenne, Habanero, Ghost Pepper, Banana Pepper, Poblano, Serrano, Anaheim, and Sweet Italian. Peppers need full sun (6-8 hours), well-drained fertile soil with pH 6.0-6.8, and 1-2 inches of water per week. Start seeds indoors 8-10 weeks before last frost — they are slow to germinate (7-14 days) and benefit from heat mats (80-85°F). Transplant outdoors only after nighttime temps stay above 55°F. Space 18-24 inches apart. Stake plants when heavy with fruit. Pick first few flowers to strengthen the plant. Hot peppers get hotter with mild water stress near harvest. Harvest by cutting (not pulling) from the plant. Days to harvest: 60-90 from transplant. Temperature: min 60°F, ideal 70-85°F. Companion plants: basil, tomatoes, carrots. Common problems: aphids, blossom end rot, sunscald.

## CUCUMBER GROWING GUIDE
Cucumber (Cucumis sativus) is a warm-season vine crop. Varieties include Slicing (Marketmore, Straight Eight), Pickling (Boston Pickling, National Pickling), English/Seedless (Telegraph), Lemon, Armenian, and Bush types for small spaces. Cucumbers need full sun (6-8 hours), rich well-drained soil with pH 6.0-7.0, and 1-2 inches of water per week (consistent moisture is critical). Direct sow after last frost when soil is 60°F+, or start indoors 3 weeks before in peat pots. Germination in 3-10 days. Space 12-18 inches apart (trellised) or 36 inches (ground). Growing vertically saves space, keeps fruit clean, and improves air circulation. Both male and female flowers are normal — female flowers have a tiny cucumber behind them. Harvest slicing types at 6-8 inches, pickling types at 2-4 inches. Bitter fruit means inconsistent watering. Days to harvest: 50-70. Temperature: min 60°F, ideal 70-85°F. Companion plants: beans, corn, peas, sunflowers. Common problems: cucumber beetles, powdery mildew, mosaic virus.

## CARROT GROWING GUIDE
Carrot (Daucus carota) is a root vegetable that must be direct sown — never transplant. Varieties include Nantes (sweet, cylindrical), Chantenay (short, stout, good for heavy soil), Danvers (classic taper), Little Finger (miniature, great for containers), Imperator (long, grocery store type), and Purple/Rainbow varieties. Carrots need full sun to partial shade (4-6 hours), deep loose sandy soil free of rocks with pH 6.0-6.8, and 1 inch of water per week. Sow tiny seeds 1/4 inch deep. Germination is slow: 14-21 days — be patient and keep soil moist. Thin to 2-3 inches apart (essential for root development). Rocks and hard soil cause forked roots. Short varieties are great for containers and heavy soil. Days to harvest: 60-80. Carrots sweeten after light frost. Harvest by loosening soil with a fork first. Temperature: min 40°F, ideal 60-70°F. Companion plants: onions, leeks, rosemary, tomatoes. Common problems: carrot fly, forked roots, green shoulders (hill soil over exposed tops).

## GREEN BEAN GROWING GUIDE
Green Bean (Phaseolus vulgaris) comes in two main types: Bush beans and Pole beans. Varieties include Blue Lake, Kentucky Wonder, Provider, Dragon Tongue, Wax Bean (yellow), Romano (flat), and Scarlet Runner. Green beans need full sun (6-8 hours), well-drained average soil with pH 6.0-7.0, and 1 inch of water per week. Always direct sow — beans don't transplant well. Plant 1-2 inches deep after last frost when soil is 60°F+. Space 4-6 inches apart. Bush beans need no support and produce over 2-3 weeks. Pole beans need trellis/poles and produce all season. Beans fix nitrogen in soil, improving it for future crops. Harvest when pods are firm and snap easily, before seeds bulge inside. Pick every 2-3 days to keep plants producing. Days to harvest: 50-65. Temperature: min 60°F, ideal 65-80°F. Companion plants: corn, squash, carrots, cucumbers. Common problems: Mexican bean beetle, rust, mosaic virus.

## STRAWBERRY GROWING GUIDE
Strawberry (Fragaria × ananassa) is a perennial fruit crop. Types include June-bearing (one large crop in June), Everbearing (2-3 crops per season), Day-neutral (continuous fruiting), and Alpine (small, intensely flavored). Popular varieties: Earliglow, Honeoye, Seascape, Albion, Ozark Beauty, and Tristar. Strawberries need full sun (6-8 hours), well-drained slightly acidic soil with pH 5.5-6.5, and 1-1.5 inches of water per week. Much easier to start from transplants than seed. If starting from seed, cold-stratify first (freeze 2-4 weeks), surface sow (needs light). Space 12-18 inches apart. Mulch with straw to keep berries clean, retain moisture, and suppress weeds. Remove flowers the first year (June-bearing) to strengthen plants. Runners can be pinned to soil for new plants or clipped. Protect from birds with netting. Days to harvest: 4-6 weeks from flowering (Year 2 for best harvest). Perennial — returns each year for 3-5 years. Temperature: min 35°F, ideal 60-80°F. Companion plants: lettuce, spinach, beans, borage. Common problems: birds, slugs, gray mold (botrytis), verticillium wilt.

## SPINACH GROWING GUIDE
Spinach (Spinacia oleracea) is a nutrient-dense cool-season leafy green. Varieties include Savoy (crinkled leaves), Flat-leaf (Smooth), Semi-savoy, and Baby Spinach types. Popular varieties: Bloomsdale Long Standing, Space, Tyee, and Regiment. Spinach needs partial to full sun (3-5 hours), rich well-drained soil with pH 6.5-7.5, and 1-1.5 inches of water per week. Direct sow 1/2 inch deep as early as 4-6 weeks before last frost. Germination in 5-14 days. Thin to 3-5 inches apart — use thinnings as microgreens. Best grown in spring and fall. Bolts quickly in summer heat above 75°F. Use shade cloth to extend harvest in warm weather. Harvest outer leaves when 3-4 inches long. Succession plant every 2 weeks. Days to harvest: 35-50. Temperature: min 35°F, ideal 50-65°F. Companion plants: strawberries, peas, beans. Common problems: bolting, downy mildew, leaf miners, aphids.

## RADISH GROWING GUIDE
Radish (Raphanus sativus) is the fastest vegetable from seed to table. Varieties include Cherry Belle, French Breakfast, Easter Egg (mixed colors), Watermelon (green outside, pink inside), Daikon (long white Asian radish), and Black Spanish. Radishes need full sun to partial shade (4-6 hours), loose well-drained soil with pH 6.0-7.0, and 1 inch of water per week. Direct sow 1/2 inch deep — can plant as soon as soil is workable, even before last frost. Germination in just 3-5 days! Thin to 2 inches apart. Harvest spring varieties when 1 inch in diameter (22-30 days). Don't let them get too big — overgrown radishes become woody and pithy. Perfect companion plant — sow between slower crops as a "row marker." Daikon types take 45-70 days and grow much larger. Temperature: min 40°F, ideal 50-65°F. Companion plants: carrots, peas, lettuce. Common problems: flea beetles, root maggots, cracking.

## MINT GROWING GUIDE
Mint (Mentha) is a vigorous perennial herb. Varieties include Peppermint, Spearmint, Chocolate Mint, Lemon Balm, Apple Mint, Pineapple Mint, and Mojito Mint. ALWAYS grow mint in containers — it spreads aggressively via underground runners and will take over garden beds. Mint needs partial to full sun (3-6 hours), any decent soil with pH 6.0-7.0, and consistent moisture. Surface sow seeds (needs light) or buy starter plants (much easier). Germination in 10-15 days. Can grow indoors year-round. The more you harvest, the bushier it grows. Pinch off flower buds to keep leaves flavorful. Nearly impossible to kill. Harvest by cutting stems just above a leaf pair. Days to established harvest: 30. Temperature: min 30°F, ideal 55-70°F. Companion plants: tomatoes, cabbage (deters pests). Common problems: rust, powdery mildew, spider mites. Divide plants every 2-3 years when they become root-bound.

## ZUCCHINI GROWING GUIDE
Zucchini (Cucurbita pepo) is one of the most productive garden vegetables. Varieties include Black Beauty, Golden Zucchini, Costata Romanesco, Eight Ball (round), Tromboncino (climbing), and Pattypan/Scallop squash (related). Zucchini needs full sun (6-8 hours), rich well-drained soil with pH 6.0-7.5, and 1-2 inches of water per week at the base of the plant. Direct sow 1 inch deep after last frost or start indoors 2-3 weeks before. Germination in 4-7 days. Space 24-36 inches apart. One plant can produce 6-10 pounds of fruit! Plant 2-3 plants max for a family. Harvest at 6-8 inches for best flavor. Check daily — zucchini grow incredibly fast. Both male and female flowers appear — male flowers appear first, female flowers have a small fruit at their base. Zucchini blossoms are edible and delicious stuffed or fried. Watch for powdery mildew — improve air circulation and water at the base. Days to harvest: 45-60. Temperature: min 60°F, ideal 70-85°F. Companion plants: corn, beans, nasturtiums. Common problems: squash bugs, vine borers, powdery mildew, blossom end rot.

## KALE GROWING GUIDE
Kale (Brassica oleracea var. sabellica) is an extremely cold-hardy, nutrient-dense leafy green. Varieties include Curly Kale (Winterbor, Redbor), Lacinato/Dinosaur Kale (Tuscan, Nero di Toscana), Red Russian, Siberian, and Ornamental Kale. Kale needs full sun to partial shade (4-6 hours), rich well-drained soil with pH 6.0-7.5, and 1-1.5 inches of water per week. Direct sow 1/2 inch deep 3-5 weeks before last frost (spring) or 6-8 weeks before first frost (fall). Germination in 5-8 days. Space 12-18 inches apart. Kale actually tastes sweeter after frost! Can survive temperatures down to 20°F. Harvest outer leaves first, leaving center to keep growing. Remove yellowing lower leaves. Days to harvest: 55-75. Great for containers. Companion plants: beets, celery, herbs, onions. Common problems: cabbage worms, aphids, clubroot. One of the most nutritious vegetables — high in vitamins A, C, K, and iron.

## PEAS GROWING GUIDE
Peas (Pisum sativum) are a cool-season legume. Types include Shelling/Garden Peas (Green Arrow), Snow Peas (Oregon Sugar Pod), and Sugar Snap Peas (Sugar Ann, Sugar Snap). Peas need full sun (6-8 hours), well-drained soil with pH 6.0-7.5, and 1 inch of water per week. Direct sow 1-2 inches deep as soon as soil can be worked in spring (4-6 weeks before last frost). Germination in 7-14 days. Inoculate seeds with nitrogen-fixing rhizobium bacteria for best results. Most varieties need a trellis or support (except bush types). Peas climb using tendrils. Harvest shelling peas when pods are plump, snow peas when flat, and snap peas when pods are full but still crisp. Peas stop producing in summer heat. Days to harvest: 55-70. Temperature: min 40°F, ideal 55-65°F. Companion plants: carrots, turnips, radishes, cucumbers. Common problems: powdery mildew, aphids, root rot. Peas fix nitrogen in soil — plant nitrogen-hungry crops (tomatoes, corn) in the same spot next year.

## ONION GROWING GUIDE
Onion (Allium cepa) is a fundamental kitchen garden crop. Types include Short-day (Vidalia, Texas Sweet — south), Long-day (Walla Walla, Yellow Sweet Spanish — north), and Day-neutral (Candy, Superstar). Also: Green Onions/Scallions, Shallots, and Leeks. Onions need full sun (6+ hours), loose well-drained fertile soil with pH 6.0-7.0, and 1 inch of water per week. Start from seed (10-12 weeks before transplant), sets (small bulbs), or transplants. Plant sets 1 inch deep, 4-6 inches apart. Choose the right day-length type for your latitude! Onions bulb based on day length. Stop watering when tops begin to fall over. Cure harvested onions in a warm, dry spot for 2-3 weeks. Days to harvest: 90-120 (bulb onions), 60-80 (green onions). Temperature: min 35°F, ideal 55-75°F. Companion plants: carrots, lettuce, tomatoes, strawberries. Common problems: onion thrips, downy mildew, neck rot.

## GARLIC GROWING GUIDE
Garlic (Allium sativum) is planted in fall for summer harvest. Types include Hardneck (better for cold climates, produces scapes, stronger flavor) and Softneck (better for mild climates, stores longer, braids well). Popular varieties: Music (hardneck), German Extra Hardy, Inchelium Red (softneck), and California Early. Plant individual cloves 2-3 inches deep, pointed end up, in fall (4-6 weeks before ground freezes). Space 6 inches apart. Mulch heavily with straw (4-6 inches) for winter protection. Garlic needs full sun, well-drained soil with pH 6.0-7.0, and 1 inch of water per week during active growth. Cut hardneck scapes in spring (edible — great in stir-fries and pesto). Harvest when lower 1/3 of leaves have browned. Cure for 2-4 weeks in a warm, dry, airy space. Store in cool, dark place. Days to harvest: 240+ (fall planted, summer harvest). Temperature: needs cold period. Companion plants: roses, tomatoes, peppers. Common problems: white rot, rust, nematodes.

## SWEET POTATO GROWING GUIDE
Sweet Potato (Ipomoea batatas) is a warm-season root crop grown from slips (rooted sprouts), not seeds. Varieties include Beauregard, Covington, Jewel, Japanese Purple, and Georgia Jet. Sweet potatoes need full sun (6-8 hours), loose sandy soil with pH 5.8-6.2, and moderate water (1 inch per week, reduce near harvest). Plant slips outdoors 2-4 weeks after last frost when soil is 65°F+. Space 12-18 inches apart in raised ridges or mounds. Vines spread 6-10 feet. Do NOT over-fertilize with nitrogen (promotes vines over tubers). Harvest before first frost — dig carefully with a fork. Cure in warm (80-85°F), humid conditions for 10-14 days to develop sweetness. Store in cool (55-60°F) dark place. Days to harvest: 90-120. Temperature: min 60°F, ideal 75-85°F. Companion plants: beans, corn. Common problems: sweet potato weevil, wireworms, deer.

## BROCCOLI GROWING GUIDE
Broccoli (Brassica oleracea var. italica) is a cool-season crop packed with nutrients. Varieties include Calabrese, De Cicco, Waltham 29, Green Magic, and Sprouting Broccoli. Broccoli needs full sun (6 hours), rich moist soil with pH 6.0-7.0, and 1-1.5 inches of water per week. Start seeds indoors 6-8 weeks before last frost (spring crop) or direct sow 85-100 days before first frost (fall crop). Germination in 5-10 days. Space 18-24 inches apart. Harvest the central head when buds are tight and before flowers open. After cutting the main head, many varieties produce smaller side shoots for weeks. Days to harvest: 55-100 from transplant. Prefers cool weather (60-70°F) — bolts (flowers) in heat above 80°F. Temperature: min 40°F, ideal 60-70°F. Companion plants: celery, onions, potatoes, dill. Common problems: cabbage worms (use row cover or BT), aphids, clubroot, black rot.

## CAULIFLOWER GROWING GUIDE
Cauliflower (Brassica oleracea var. botrytis) is more challenging than broccoli but very rewarding. Varieties include Snowball, Purple of Sicily, Cheddar (orange), Graffiti (purple), and Romanesco (fractal spirals). Cauliflower needs full sun (6 hours), very rich moist soil with pH 6.0-7.0, and consistent moisture (1.5-2 inches per week). Start seeds indoors 6-8 weeks before last frost. Germination in 5-10 days. Space 24 inches apart. Cauliflower does not tolerate temperature extremes — any stress causes it to "button" (form tiny premature heads). Blanch white varieties by tying outer leaves over the developing head when it's 2-3 inches across. Harvest when heads are 6-8 inches across and still compact. Days to harvest: 55-100 from transplant. Temperature: ideal 60-70°F. Companion plants: beans, celery, spinach. Common problems: premature heading, cabbage worms, black rot.

## EGGPLANT GROWING GUIDE
Eggplant (Solanum melongena) is a warm-season crop related to tomatoes and peppers. Varieties include Black Beauty, Ichiban (Japanese, long/thin), Rosa Bianca (Italian heirloom), Fairy Tale (small striped), and Thai Green. Eggplant needs full sun (6-8 hours), fertile well-drained soil with pH 5.5-6.5, and 1-2 inches of water per week. Start seeds indoors 8-10 weeks before last frost. Needs warm conditions (80°F) for germination (10-14 days). Transplant outdoors when nighttime temps stay above 60°F. Space 18-24 inches apart. Stake plants when heavy with fruit. Harvest when skin is glossy and bounces back slightly when pressed. Overripe fruit has dull skin and brown seeds. Days to harvest: 65-80 from transplant. Temperature: min 60°F, ideal 75-85°F. Companion plants: beans, peppers, spinach. Common problems: flea beetles, Colorado potato beetle, verticillium wilt.

## CORN GROWING GUIDE
Corn (Zea mays) needs space but is very satisfying to grow. Types include Sweet Corn (Silver Queen, Peaches and Cream, Honey Select), Popcorn, Flour Corn, and Ornamental. Corn needs full sun (8+ hours), rich soil with pH 6.0-6.8, and 1.5-2 inches of water per week. Direct sow 1-1.5 inches deep after last frost when soil is 60°F+. Plant in blocks (at least 4 rows) rather than single rows for proper wind pollination. Space 8-12 inches apart, rows 30-36 inches apart. Corn is a heavy feeder — side-dress with nitrogen when plants are knee-high. Harvest sweet corn when silks are brown and dried, kernels are plump and milky when pierced. Days to harvest: 60-100. Temperature: min 60°F, ideal 75-86°F. Companion plants: beans, squash (the "Three Sisters"). Common problems: corn earworm, raccoons, wind lodging. Tip: Plant succession crops every 2 weeks for extended harvest.

## SQUASH (WINTER) GROWING GUIDE
Winter Squash includes Butternut, Acorn, Spaghetti Squash, Delicata, Hubbard, Kabocha, and Pumpkin varieties. Despite the name, winter squash is planted in summer and stored through winter. Needs full sun (6-8 hours), rich soil with pH 6.0-7.0, and 1-2 inches of water per week. Direct sow 1 inch deep after last frost in hills or mounds. Space 3-5 feet apart (these are large vining plants). Germination in 5-10 days. Harvest when skin is hard (can't be dented with a fingernail) and the vine stem is dry. Leave 2-3 inches of stem attached. Cure in warm (80°F) dry spot for 10-14 days (except delicata — eat fresh). Store in cool (50-55°F) dry place for months. Days to harvest: 80-120. Temperature: min 60°F, ideal 70-85°F. Companion plants: corn, beans, nasturtiums. Common problems: squash vine borer, squash bugs, powdery mildew. Tip: Slip cardboard under developing fruit to prevent rot.

## HERB GARDEN GUIDE
Popular herbs beyond basil and mint include: Rosemary (perennial, full sun, well-drained soil, drought tolerant), Thyme (perennial, full sun, dry soil, great ground cover), Oregano (perennial, full sun, well-drained soil, Mediterranean cuisine), Cilantro/Coriander (annual, partial shade, cool weather, bolts in heat — plant in fall/spring), Parsley (biennial, partial to full sun, rich moist soil, flat-leaf has more flavor), Dill (annual, full sun, average soil, self-sows freely, attracts beneficial insects), Sage (perennial, full sun, well-drained soil, drought tolerant once established), Chives (perennial, full sun to partial shade, any soil, edible purple flowers), Lavender (perennial, full sun, sandy well-drained alkaline soil, drought tolerant), and Tarragon (perennial, full sun, well-drained soil, French tarragon is the culinary variety). Most herbs prefer well-drained soil and full sun. Many are Mediterranean and prefer slightly dry conditions. Herbs can be grown in containers, window boxes, or dedicated herb gardens. Harvest herbs in the morning when essential oils are strongest.

## WATERMELON GROWING GUIDE
Watermelon (Citrullus lanatus) is a warm-season vine crop. Varieties include Crimson Sweet, Sugar Baby (small/icebox type), Charleston Gray, Yellow Doll, and Seedless types. Watermelons need full sun (8+ hours), sandy well-drained soil with pH 6.0-6.8, and 1-2 inches of water per week (reduce as fruit ripens). Start seeds indoors 3-4 weeks before last frost or direct sow when soil is 70°F+. Germination in 4-12 days. Space 3-6 feet apart. Vines spread 10-15 feet. Pollination is by bees — plant flowers nearby to attract pollinators. Fruit is ripe when: the bottom spot turns yellow, the tendril nearest the fruit dries up, and the melon sounds hollow when tapped. Days to harvest: 70-90. Temperature: min 65°F, ideal 75-90°F. Companion plants: corn, sunflowers, nasturtiums. Common problems: fusarium wilt, anthracnose, cucumber beetles.

## POTATO GROWING GUIDE
Potato (Solanum tuberosum) is grown from seed potatoes (certified disease-free tubers), not true seeds. Varieties include Russet (baking), Yukon Gold (all-purpose), Red Pontiac, Fingerling (French), Purple/Blue, and New Potatoes (any harvested young). Potatoes need full sun (6-8 hours), loose acidic soil with pH 5.0-6.0, and 1-2 inches of water per week. Plant seed potatoes (or pieces with 2-3 eyes) 4 inches deep, 12 inches apart, after last frost when soil is 45°F+. Hill soil around stems as plants grow (this is where tubers form). Stop hilling when plants flower. Harvest new potatoes when plants flower; harvest mature potatoes 2-3 weeks after vines die back. Let skins cure for a few days before storage. Days to harvest: 70-120. Temperature: min 45°F, ideal 60-70°F. Companion plants: beans, corn, cabbage, horseradish. Common problems: Colorado potato beetle, late blight, scab. NEVER eat green potatoes or potato berries — they contain toxic solanine.

## SUNFLOWER GROWING GUIDE
Sunflower (Helianthus annuus) is easy, rewarding, and great for pollinators. Varieties include Mammoth (10+ feet), Dwarf Sunspot, Teddy Bear (fluffy double blooms), Italian White, and multi-branching types. Sunflowers need full sun (6-8 hours), average well-drained soil with pH 6.0-7.5, and moderate water. Direct sow 1 inch deep after last frost. Germination in 7-10 days. Space 6-12 inches apart (small types) or 18-24 inches (giant types). Very fast growing. Tall varieties may need staking. Deadhead multi-branching types for continued blooms. Leave seed heads for birds in fall, or harvest seeds when the back of the head turns brown. Days to bloom: 55-70. Temperature: min 50°F, ideal 70-78°F. Companion plants: corn, beans, squash, cucumbers. Common problems: squirrels/birds (eating seeds), aphids, downy mildew.

## SWISS CHARD GROWING GUIDE
Swiss Chard (Beta vulgaris subsp. vulgaris) is a colorful, heat-tolerant leafy green related to beets. Varieties include Bright Lights (rainbow stems), Fordhook Giant, Ruby Red, and Golden. Chard needs full sun to partial shade (4-6 hours), rich well-drained soil with pH 6.0-7.0, and 1 inch of water per week. Direct sow 1/2 inch deep 2-3 weeks before last frost. Germination in 5-14 days. Space 8-12 inches apart. Unlike lettuce and spinach, chard tolerates summer heat without bolting. Harvest outer leaves when 6-8 inches tall — plant keeps producing from the center. Both leaves and stems are edible (cook stems longer). Can produce for entire growing season. Days to harvest: 50-60. Temperature: min 40°F, ideal 60-75°F. Companion plants: beans, cabbage, onions. Common problems: leaf miners, slugs, downy mildew.

## BEET GROWING GUIDE
Beet (Beta vulgaris) is a dual-purpose crop — both roots and greens are edible. Varieties include Detroit Dark Red, Golden (yellow, doesn't stain), Chioggia (candy-striped inside), Bull's Blood (deep red greens), and Cylindra (cylindrical shape). Beets need full sun to partial shade (4-6 hours), loose well-drained soil with pH 6.0-7.5, and 1 inch of water per week. Direct sow 1/2 inch deep 2-4 weeks before last frost. Each "seed" is actually a cluster containing 2-4 seeds, so thinning is essential. Thin to 3-4 inches apart. Germination in 5-14 days. Harvest greens anytime; harvest roots when 1.5-3 inches in diameter. Days to harvest: 50-70. Temperature: min 40°F, ideal 60-70°F. Companion plants: onions, lettuce, cabbage family. Common problems: cercospora leaf spot, scab, root maggots. Tip: Soak seeds overnight before planting to speed germination.

## ASPARAGUS GROWING GUIDE
Asparagus (Asparagus officinalis) is a perennial crop that produces for 15-20+ years once established. Varieties include Mary Washington, Jersey Giant, and Purple Passion. Asparagus needs full sun (8+ hours), deep well-drained soil with pH 6.5-7.5, and 1-1.5 inches of water per week. Plant 1-year-old crowns (roots) in trenches 6-8 inches deep, 18 inches apart. Do NOT harvest the first year; harvest lightly the second year; full harvest (6-8 week season) from year three onward. Harvest spears when 6-8 inches tall by snapping at the base. Stop harvesting when spears become thin (pencil-size). Allow ferns to grow all summer — they feed the roots for next year. Cut back dead ferns in late fall or early spring. Days to first harvest: 2-3 years. Temperature: needs winter dormancy (freezing temperatures). Companion plants: tomatoes, parsley, basil.

## COMPANION PLANTING GUIDE
Companion planting is the practice of growing certain plants together for mutual benefit. Key principles: Some plants repel pests that attack their companions. Some plants attract beneficial insects (predators, pollinators). Some plants improve soil for their neighbors. Some plants provide physical support or shade. Classic combinations: The Three Sisters (corn, beans, squash) — corn provides bean support, beans fix nitrogen, squash leaves shade soil. Tomatoes + Basil — basil may repel tomato hornworms and improve flavor. Carrots + Onions — onions repel carrot fly, carrots repel onion fly. Marigolds planted throughout the garden repel many pests and nematodes. Nasturtiums act as trap crops for aphids. Avoid planting: Tomatoes near brassicas (broccoli, cabbage). Onions/garlic near beans/peas. Dill near carrots (cross-pollination). Fennel near most vegetables (inhibits growth).

## PEST MANAGEMENT GUIDE
Common garden pests and organic solutions: Aphids — small soft-bodied insects in clusters. Spray with strong water jet, use insecticidal soap, or release ladybugs. Tomato Hornworm — large green caterpillar. Hand-pick (look for frass/droppings), plant dill to attract parasitic wasps. Cabbage Worms — green caterpillars on brassicas. Use floating row covers, apply BT (Bacillus thuringiensis), hand-pick. Slugs/Snails — use beer traps, diatomaceous earth, copper tape, or hand-pick at night. Japanese Beetles — hand-pick into soapy water, use milky spore for grubs, plant trap crops. Squash Vine Borer — wrap stem base with aluminum foil, inject BT into affected stems, plant resistant varieties. Cucumber Beetles — use row covers until flowering, hand-pick, plant trap crops (blue Hubbard squash). Flea Beetles — tiny jumping beetles that make shotgun-hole patterns. Use row covers, diatomaceous earth. Spider Mites — tiny dots on leaf undersides. Increase humidity, spray with water, use neem oil. Colorado Potato Beetle — yellow/black striped. Hand-pick adults and orange egg masses, rotate crops. General IPM (Integrated Pest Management): Encourage beneficial insects (ladybugs, lacewings, parasitic wasps). Rotate crops yearly. Keep garden clean of debris. Use row covers as physical barriers. Apply organic sprays (neem oil, insecticidal soap) only as needed.

## DISEASE PREVENTION GUIDE
Common plant diseases and management: Powdery Mildew — white powdery coating on leaves. Improve air circulation, avoid overhead watering, spray with diluted milk (1:9) or neem oil. Blight (Early and Late) — brown spots on tomato/potato leaves. Use resistant varieties, rotate crops, remove affected leaves, apply copper fungicide preventively. Damping Off — seedlings fall over and die at soil level. Use sterile seed starting mix, don't overwater, ensure good air circulation. Fusarium/Verticillium Wilt — plants wilt despite adequate water. Use resistant varieties (marked F or V), rotate crops, solarize soil. Blossom End Rot — dark sunken spots on tomato/pepper/squash bottoms. Not a disease — caused by calcium deficiency due to inconsistent watering. Water evenly, mulch heavily, add calcium (crusite or gypsum). Root Rot — plants wilt, roots are brown/mushy. Improve drainage, avoid overwatering, use raised beds. Mosaic Virus — mottled yellow/green leaves, stunted growth. No cure — remove affected plants, control aphids (vectors), wash hands/tools. General prevention: Rotate crops on a 3-4 year cycle. Space plants properly for air circulation. Water at the base, not from above. Use disease-resistant varieties when available. Clean tools between plants. Remove and destroy (don't compost) diseased plant material.

## SOIL HEALTH GUIDE
Good soil is the foundation of a successful garden. Soil Types: Sandy (drains fast, warms quickly, low nutrients — add compost), Clay (drains slowly, compacts, high nutrients — add organic matter and gypsum), Loam (ideal mix of sand, silt, clay — most gardens aim for this), and Silt (fertile but compacts when wet — add compost). Soil pH: Most vegetables prefer 6.0-7.0. Test with a home kit or send to your county extension office. Raise pH with lime, lower with sulfur or peat moss. Organic Matter: Add 2-3 inches of compost annually. Compost improves all soil types. Composting: Mix green (nitrogen-rich: kitchen scraps, grass clippings) and brown (carbon-rich: leaves, cardboard, straw) materials. Keep moist, turn regularly. Ready in 2-6 months. Vermicomposting (worm bins) works great indoors. Mulching: Apply 2-4 inches of organic mulch (straw, leaves, wood chips) to suppress weeds, retain moisture, and regulate soil temperature. Don't let mulch touch plant stems. Cover Crops: Plant in fall to protect and enrich soil. Crimson clover, winter rye, and hairy vetch fix nitrogen and prevent erosion. Raised Beds: Great for poor soil, drainage issues, accessibility, and extending the season. Fill with a mix of topsoil, compost, and peat/coir. No-Till/No-Dig: Minimize soil disturbance to preserve soil structure and beneficial organisms. Layer compost on top rather than digging it in.

## WATERING GUIDE
Proper watering is crucial for garden success. General rule: most vegetables need 1-1.5 inches of water per week (including rain). Deep, infrequent watering encourages deep root growth. Shallow, frequent watering causes shallow roots. Water early in the morning to reduce evaporation and disease. Water at the base of plants, not from overhead (reduces fungal disease). Signs of overwatering: yellowing leaves, wilting despite wet soil, root rot, fungus gnats. Signs of underwatering: wilting (especially in afternoon heat), dry crispy leaf edges, slow growth, blossom drop. Drip irrigation is the most efficient method — delivers water directly to roots with minimal waste. Soaker hoses work well for row gardens. Mulch reduces water needs by 25-50%. Self-watering containers are great for balcony gardens and vacation coverage. Rain barrels collect free water — check local regulations. Container plants need more frequent watering than in-ground plants. Clay pots dry out faster than plastic. Check soil moisture by sticking your finger 1-2 inches into the soil — water when dry at that depth.

## SEED STARTING GUIDE
Starting seeds indoors gives you a head start on the growing season. Equipment needed: seed starting trays or small pots, seed starting mix (NOT garden soil), grow lights or very sunny south-facing window, heat mat (for warm-season crops), spray bottle, labels. Steps: Fill containers with moist seed starting mix. Plant seeds at the depth indicated on the packet (general rule: 2x the seed diameter). Cover with plastic wrap or humidity dome to retain moisture. Keep warm (65-85°F depending on crop). Remove cover once seeds germinate. Provide 14-16 hours of light per day (supplemental grow lights are strongly recommended). Water from the bottom by setting trays in water. Fertilize with half-strength liquid fertilizer when true leaves appear. Harden off seedlings by gradually exposing them to outdoor conditions over 7-10 days before transplanting. Cool-season crops to start indoors: broccoli, cauliflower, kale, lettuce. Warm-season crops to start indoors: tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, basil. Crops to direct sow (do NOT start indoors): beans, peas, carrots, radishes, corn, squash, cucumbers (or start in peat pots to avoid root disturbance).

## CONTAINER GARDENING GUIDE
Almost any vegetable can be grown in containers with the right setup. Container size matters: herbs need 6-8 inch pots, lettuce/greens need 6-12 inch pots, tomatoes/peppers need 5+ gallon containers, root vegetables need 12+ inch depth. Use potting mix (NOT garden soil — too heavy and compacts in containers). Ensure drainage holes in all containers. Self-watering containers reduce maintenance. Container plants need more frequent watering and fertilizing than in-ground plants. Feed every 1-2 weeks with liquid fertilizer during growing season. Best vegetables for containers: tomatoes (dwarf/determinate varieties), lettuce, herbs (all), peppers, radishes, green beans (bush type), strawberries, green onions, spinach, and chard. Materials: plastic (lightweight, retains moisture), terracotta/clay (breathable but dries fast, heavy), fabric grow bags (excellent drainage and root air-pruning), wood (insulates well), metal (gets hot in sun — insulate or use light colors). Balcony considerations: weight limits (wet soil is heavy), wind exposure (protect or stake plants), sun exposure (track hours of direct sun), and drainage (use saucers to protect floors).

## SEASONAL PLANTING CALENDAR
Early Spring (4-6 weeks before last frost): Direct sow peas, spinach, radishes, lettuce, kale, carrots, beets, onion sets. Start indoors: tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, broccoli, cauliflower. Mid Spring (around last frost): Transplant cool-season seedlings outdoors. Continue sowing cool-season crops. Late Spring (after last frost): Direct sow beans, corn, squash, cucumbers, melons. Transplant tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, basil outdoors. Summer: Succession plant lettuce, radishes, beans every 2-3 weeks. Start fall brassicas indoors. Late Summer (6-8 weeks before first frost): Direct sow fall crops: spinach, lettuce, radishes, kale, turnips, peas. Fall: Plant garlic and shallots. Plant cover crops. Harvest winter squash and root vegetables. Mulch perennial herbs and strawberries. Prepare and rest garden beds. Winter: Plan next year's garden. Order seeds from catalogs. Start onion seeds indoors (January-February). Maintain indoor herbs. Clean and sharpen tools. Build/repair raised beds and trellises.

## HARVESTING AND STORAGE GUIDE
Harvest tips for maximum flavor and nutrition: Pick in the morning after dew dries for best flavor. Use clean sharp tools. Handle produce gently to avoid bruising. Refrigerate most vegetables quickly after harvest. Storage methods: Root cellar/cool storage (40-50°F, high humidity): potatoes, carrots, beets, turnips, cabbage, apples. Cure first: onions, garlic, winter squash, sweet potatoes. Countertop (room temp): tomatoes (never refrigerate — kills flavor), avocados, bananas, melons (until ripe). Refrigerator: most leafy greens, herbs (in damp paper towel), peppers, cucumbers, beans, broccoli. Preserving: Canning (water bath for high-acid foods like tomatoes, pickles; pressure canning for low-acid foods). Freezing (blanch most vegetables first). Dehydrating (herbs, tomatoes, peppers, fruit). Fermentation (sauerkraut, kimchi, pickles). Saving seeds: Let fruit fully mature/dry on plant. Collect, clean, and dry seeds. Store in cool, dry, dark place. Label with variety and date. Most seeds remain viable 2-5 years. Open-pollinated/heirloom varieties breed true from seed; hybrid (F1) seeds don't.

## ORGANIC GARDENING PRINCIPLES
Organic gardening avoids synthetic chemicals and works with nature. Core principles: Build healthy soil with compost and organic matter. Use crop rotation to prevent disease buildup. Encourage biodiversity — plant flowers, herbs, and native plants to attract beneficial insects. Practice IPM (Integrated Pest Management) — prevention first, then physical/biological controls, organic sprays as last resort. Approved organic fertilizers: compost, fish emulsion, bone meal, blood meal, kelp/seaweed, worm castings. Approved pest controls: neem oil, insecticidal soap, BT (Bacillus thuringiensis), diatomaceous earth, pyrethrin (derived from chrysanthemums), copper fungicide. Attract beneficial insects: ladybugs (eat aphids), lacewings (eat aphids, mites), parasitic wasps (control caterpillars), ground beetles (eat slugs), praying mantis. Plant insectary strips with dill, fennel, yarrow, sweet alyssum, and marigolds. Pollinators: Plant bee-friendly flowers. Avoid pesticide spraying during bloom. Provide water sources. Leave some bare soil for ground-nesting bees.

## RAISED BED GARDENING
Raised beds are an excellent way to garden, especially if you have poor soil, drainage issues, or physical limitations. Benefits: Better drainage, earlier soil warming in spring, less compaction (never walk on the soil), easier weed management, more accessible (build at any height), and defined growing space. Size: 4 feet wide maximum (to reach the center from both sides), any length. Height: 6-12 inches for most crops, 18-24 inches for root vegetables or wheelchair accessibility. Materials: Untreated wood (cedar or redwood last longest), composite lumber, stone/brick, concrete blocks, galvanized steel. Avoid treated wood (chemicals can leach into soil). Fill with a mix of 60% topsoil, 30% compost, 10% perlite/vermiculite. Or use the "Mel's Mix" from Square Foot Gardening: 1/3 peat/coir, 1/3 compost (from 5+ sources), 1/3 vermiculite. Top-dress with 1-2 inches of compost annually. Intensive planting in raised beds yields 2-5x more per square foot than traditional rows.

## GROWING FOOD IN SMALL SPACES
Urban and small-space gardening techniques: Vertical gardening — use trellises, wall-mounted planters, stacking pots, and hanging baskets. Grow vining crops (tomatoes, cucumbers, beans, peas) vertically. Window boxes — perfect for herbs, lettuce, and small flowers. Balcony containers — use lightweight pots, check weight limits, maximize sun exposure. Indoor growing — herbs on a sunny windowsill, microgreens under grow lights, lettuce under LED grow lights. Community gardens — shared plots available in most cities. Check local parks department or search community garden networks. Rooftop gardens — check structural capacity, use lightweight containers, and plan for wind exposure. Square foot gardening — divide raised beds into 1-foot squares, plant specific numbers per square based on plant size. Succession planting — replant empty squares immediately after harvest for continuous production. Interplanting — grow fast crops (radishes, lettuce) between slow crops (tomatoes, peppers) to maximize space usage.

## CLIMATE AND ZONE INFORMATION
USDA Plant Hardiness Zones are based on average annual minimum winter temperature. Find your zone at planthardiness.ars.usda.gov by entering your zip code. Zones range from 1 (coldest, -60°F) to 13 (warmest, 70°F). Your zone determines: which perennials survive your winters, approximate first and last frost dates, and the length of your growing season. Frost dates are crucial: Last spring frost date = when to transplant warm-season crops outdoors. First fall frost date = when to harvest tender crops and plant garlic. Growing season length varies from 90 days (Zone 3) to year-round (Zones 10-13). Extend your season with: cold frames, row covers, hoop houses, greenhouses, and container plants you can move indoors. Microclimates in your yard: south-facing walls retain heat, low spots collect cold air (frost pockets), urban areas are warmer than rural, and wind exposure increases cold damage.

## FREQUENTLY ASKED GARDENING QUESTIONS
Q: When should I start my garden? A: Start cool-season crops 4-6 weeks before your last frost date. Start warm-season crops indoors 6-8 weeks before last frost, transplant outdoors after frost danger passes. Your local extension service can provide exact dates for your area.

Q: How much sun does my garden need? A: Most vegetables need 6-8 hours of direct sunlight. Leafy greens and herbs can manage with 4-6 hours. Track your sun exposure over a full day before choosing a garden spot.

Q: What should a beginner grow first? A: Start with easy, forgiving crops: radishes (fastest), lettuce, herbs (basil, mint), green beans, tomatoes (cherry types), and zucchini. These are rewarding for beginners and hard to fail with.

Q: How do I know when to water? A: Stick your finger 1-2 inches into the soil. If dry at that depth, water deeply. Most vegetables need about 1 inch of water per week. Morning watering is best.

Q: Why are my plants yellowing? A: Common causes: overwatering, underwatering, nitrogen deficiency, root problems, or natural aging of lower leaves. Check soil moisture first, then consider fertilizing with nitrogen-rich amendment.

Q: What's the easiest way to improve my soil? A: Add 2-3 inches of compost on top of your garden every year. Compost improves every soil type — it loosens clay, adds moisture retention to sand, and feeds beneficial soil organisms.

Q: Can I garden without a yard? A: Absolutely! Use containers on a balcony, patio, or windowsill. Join a community garden. Grow microgreens and herbs indoors under simple LED lights. Many productive crops grow well in containers.

Q: How do I deal with pests organically? A: Start with prevention: healthy soil, proper spacing, crop rotation. Use physical barriers (row covers). Hand-pick large pests. Attract beneficial insects with flowers. Use organic sprays (neem oil, insecticidal soap) as a last resort.
